# Commercial COBOL Modernization Landscape (as of October 2026) — Incumbents, Claims, Pricing, Market, Acquisitions, Regulatory Evidence

Scope note: research ran ~45 min, ~40 tool calls. Items dated before 2025 are marked. Several primary pages (AWS re:Post guide, IBM WCA4Z docs, FFIEC DA&M booklet, Tracxn) were blocked (403/robots); findings for those come from secondary sources and are flagged.

## KQ1. Products, by approach — who ships what

### Takeaway
Three camps are real and distinct: (1) hyperscaler/IBM LLM-assisted translators that are free or bundled and sold as "accelerators" with humans in the loop; (2) a dozen rule-based translators, mostly small, mostly already acquired or partner-bound, that claim "100% automated" and "functional equivalence" but publish no audited numbers; (3) COBOL-on-JVM/.NET compilers (Rocket, Veryant, Heirloom, Raincode) whose central argument is "don't translate at all." A verification-first camp (Mechanical Orchard, Google Dual Run, Hypercubic) is emerging and is the closest to the proposed differentiator.

### Inferences
- The "translate vs compile-to-JVM" debate is really about *what you get afterwards*: compile-to-JVM (Rocket/Veryant) keeps COBOL skills in the loop and is the lowest-risk exit from Z hardware/MLC pricing; translation (Heirloom/TSRI/CloudFrame) produces non-idiomatic Java the buyer still cannot maintain idiomatically; "idiomatic" output is what LLM vendors and Mechanical Orchard promise but cannot prove. A deterministic translator with provenance sits between: its honest pitch is "JOBOL you can prove, with every line traceable," not "idiomatic Java."
- The emerging consensus (Google Dual Run, AWS Transform testing, Mechanical Orchard's byte-for-byte gate, Heirloom's rhetoric) is that *equivalence evidence is the product*, but every incumbent bolts it on as a separate test harness rather than a compiler-emitted artifact. No incumbent found advertises line-level provenance from COBOL to Java as a first-class output.

- Could not fetch IBM's WCA4Z product docs (403) to confirm the exact mechanics of the "Validation Assistant" (record/replay? what it compares).
- No reliable current status for COBOL-IT, Fujitsu NetCOBOL modernization offerings, or Raincode's 2025-26 commercial state beyond marketplace listings.
- Mechanical Orchard's "99% coverage" and "byte for byte" claims are vendor/analyst-relayed, no independent audit found.

## KQ2. Correctness: what is claimed vs what is delivered as evidence

### Takeaway
Everyone claims "functional equivalence"; nobody publishes audited pass/fail numbers. Evidence delivered ranges from nothing (most rule-based vendors) to generated test plans and data-collection JCL (AWS), replay-and-compare of production events (Google Dual Run, Mechanical Orchard), LLM-as-judge scoring (IBM, internal). The only vendor-independent quantitative statement found is Gartner's forecast that >70% of 2026 mainframe-exit projects will fail to deliver benefits because GenAI capability was overestimated.

### Cited Findings
- IBM: "Validation: Automated testing to ensure refactored code maintains semantic equivalence" but "No specific accuracy percentages provided" — [CROZ, Nov 2025](https://croz.net/honest-take-on-watsonx-code-assistant-for-z/); IBM Research's quality framework uses analytic checkers plus LLM-as-Judge, and the authors state "none of the evaluation techniques achieves perfection independently"; no aggregate correctness rate published — [arXiv 2507.23356](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23356v1)
- AWS Transform testing (Dec 2025): generates a functional test plan "based on the dependencies of programs, data, jobs, and schedulers," JCL "to collect input and output data from mainframe data stores, including sequential files, VSAM, and Db2," and scripts that "compare the results"; but "it does not manage actual mainframe job submission or data transfer"; "focus on batch workloads"; CICS/online coverage "undefined" — [Konishi analysis](https://hidekazu-konishi.com/entry/aws_transform_automation_boundaries.html); AWS's own post "provides no technical details about what baseline metrics or criteria are compared" and names no customers — [AWS News Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/aws-transform-for-mainframe-introduces-reimagine-capabilities-and-automated-testing-functionality/)
- A Blu Age user review (Oct 2023, older): "Business logic or code might give other results after conversion," asks the tool to "automate test cases within the tool to know the success rates," advises it be "avoided for the financial domain applications" — [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/mainframe-modernization-with-aws-blu-age/reviews)
- Google Dual Run: "Replay live production mainframe events onto modernized cloud application; Compare outputs between systems to detect differences; Ensure functional equivalence before production migration" — [Google Cloud, Apr 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/infrastructure-modernization/accelerate-mainframe-modernization-with-google-cloud-ai); technology originated as Santander's "Gravity"; at launch (Oct 2022) "details on how exactly this will work were scant" — [The Register, Oct 2022 (older)](https://www.theregister.com/2022/10/12/google_cloud_dual_run_service/)
- Heirloom: "100% correct" "by construction," "byte-for-byte equivalence," "direct validation using original test cases"; PHEAA case cites "zero disruption" and "succeeded on the first attempt" but "specific testing methodologies or quality metrics are not detailed" — [Heirloom](https://heirloomcomputing.com/llms-and-cobol-modernization/), [Heirloom PHEAA](https://heirloomcomputing.com/portfolio/pheaa/)
- CloudFrame/GFT: "Functional equivalence guaranteed," "Automated JUnit test case generation," SonarQube "A" — [GFT, Oct 2023 (older)](https://www.gft.com/us/en/about-us/news/press-releases/press-and-news/2023/press-releases/gft-pr-say-goodbye-to-cobol-usa)
- Mechanical Orchard: byte-for-byte match against live data on the actual LPAR; separate writer vs validator models; HFS flags the "last mile" risk when the pipeline hits "unlicensed black-box utilities requiring manual coding" — [HFS](https://www.hfsresearch.com/research/hfs-services-as-software-hot-tech-mechanical-orchard/)
- ISG's analyst view (Mar 2024): "GenAI can only write pieces of code per a programmer's prompt and cannot replace rewriting and reengineering tools that consistently generate millions of lines of code (LOC) in minutes"; practical GenAI uses are rule explanation, summarization, test-case generation — [ISG 2024](https://pages.awscloud.com/rs/112-TZM-766/images/24-GLOBAL-en-US-isg-other-ardm-isg-mainframe-application-modernization-software-reprint.pdf)
- Gartner (Jun 18, 2026): ">70% of mainframe exit projects initiated in 2026 will fail to produce intended benefits"; "By 2030, 75% of mainframe exit vendors will pivot or cease operations"; cause: "widening gap between the marketing promise of GenAI and its real-world ability to transform and migrate complex legacy code"; analyst Alessandro Galimberti — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-06-18-gartner-predicts-more-than-70-percent-of-mainframe-exit-projects-will-fail-due-to-overestimation-of-generative-ais-capabilities); The Register adds Gartner's view that GenAI tools fail on "performance and throughput matching" and "interconnected complexity and massive data volumes" — [The Register, Apr 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/15/gartner_mainframe_exit_analysis/)
- Concrete semantic traps named by practitioners: REDEFINES over packed decimals mistranslated as integers; COMP-3 to `double` rounding; `BigDecimal` needing explicit rounding mode to match COBOL ROUNDED; JCL/CA-7/TWS dependency networks unmapped; proposed remedy is "golden datasets" replayed against new code — [Veriprajna blog (vendor piece)](https://medium.com/@ashutosh_veriprajna/the-cobol-migration-that-compiled-perfectly-and-still-corrupted-the-ledger-598aef136ac4)
- Aggregator claim of "93%+ AI conversion accuracy for COBOL-to-Java" attributed to DreamFactory — [Keyhole Software](https://keyholesoftware.com/legacy-modernization-trends/) — unverified marketing figure, no methodology; treat as unreliable
- "No vendor claims 100% correctness; post-conversion review and testing are universally required" — [IN-COM 2025/26](https://www.in-com.com/blog/top-cobol-modernization-vendors-in-2025-2026-from-legacy-to-cloud/) (note: contradicted by Heirloom's and GFT/CloudFrame's own marketing above)

### Inferences
- "Functional equivalence" in this market is a *process promise* (we will test until it matches) not a *compiler guarantee*. A tool that emits the equivalence oracle (input/output capture harness + diff) and a provenance map as compiler artifacts would be unique in kind, not just degree.
- The AWS handoff boundary (customer's sysprogs must run the JCL and move data) is the same boundary a small vendor would face; it is not a disadvantage specific to being small.
- Published failure/audit numbers do not exist at vendor level. Gartner's 70% is the only number a buyer will have heard; a small vendor can position directly against it.

### Gaps
- No published differential-testing result (N inputs, M mismatches) from any vendor.
- No independent audit (Big Four or regulator) of a COBOL-to-Java translation found.

## KQ3. Pricing and sales motion

### Takeaway
Pricing is per converted line of code, per runtime (MIPS/cores/users), or a mix; hyperscalers now give the translator away and monetize the cloud. Deals are services-heavy multi-year programs sold to the CIO/transformation office, with SIs doing delivery.

### Cited Findings
- ISG: "Commercial models include charging by converted lines of code (LOC), by the runtime, by users or a combination of those... Pricing by LOC can become expensive for large portfolios, and runtime fees can accumulate after modernization" — [ISG 2024](https://pages.awscloud.com/rs/112-TZM-766/images/24-GLOBAL-en-US-isg-other-ardm-isg-mainframe-application-modernization-software-reprint.pdf)
- Cost range "$1–$25 per line of code (replatforming low-end; full-service refactoring high-end)"; durations "3–12 months (isolated); 2–5 years (enterprise-scale)" — [IN-COM 2025/26](https://www.in-com.com/blog/top-cobol-modernization-vendors-in-2025-2026-from-legacy-to-cloud/)
- Per-line conversion services "$1.50 to $4.00 per line"; "200,000-line codebase estimated cost: $300K to $800K"; enterprise platforms (TSRI, Micro Focus, Blu Age, Heirloom) cited at "$2.2 million over 12-24 months" — [Mecanik blog, Mar 2026 (small-vendor marketing)](https://medium.com/@mecanik1337/cobol-migration-in-2026-a-3-000-desktop-tool-vs-six-figure-consulting-engagements-9446d307be2b)

### Inferences
- Software is now a loss-leader for cloud consumption (AWS, Google) or consulting (IBM). A small vendor cannot win on price of translation; it can win on price of *assurance* (sold to risk/audit, or as a per-program certification fee) or on being the engine an SI resells.
- Services-to-software ratio: the $75M Astadia price (an SI boutique) vs $2.28B for the Micro Focus runtime business shows where durable value sits: runtimes and installed base, not conversion tooling.

### Gaps
- No public typical deal size for WCA4Z, AWS Transform (paid tiers), or Heirloom licenses.
- No source quantified services-to-software ratio directly.

## KQ4. Market size, notable projects and failures, IBM's position

### Takeaway
Mainframe-modernization spend is ~$9B (2025/26) inside a ~$25–30B legacy-modernization market, but IBM Z just had its best revenue year in two decades and Gartner says most exits will fail; the market is bifurcating into "modernize in place" (IBM's interest) and selective exits.

### Cited Findings

## KQ6. Regulatory and audit angle

### Takeaway
Regulators demand demonstrable, independently overseen testing of calculations and logic with audit trails, rollback plans and go/no-go discipline; none found asks for "byte-for-byte equivalence on production inputs" by name. The TSB enforcement is the de facto standard: reduced test scope, compressed/parallelized test phases and testing finished the day before cutover were the cited failings.

### Cited Findings
- FFIEC Audit booklet: "As each stage of the life cycle is reached, the auditor should review the internal controls, testing, and audit trails included in the application"; post-implementation review "should include extensive testing of program logic, calculations, error conditions, edits, and controls" — [FFIEC IT Handbook, Audit](https://ithandbook.ffiec.gov/it-booklets/audit/audit-participation-in-application-development,-acquisition,-conversions,-and-testing.aspx)
- FFIEC Development, Acquisition and Maintenance booklet last updated Aug 29, 2024 — [FDIC FIL 2024](https://fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2024/updated-ffiec-it-examination-handbook-development) (booklet text itself blocked by robots.txt)
- TSB/PRA-FCA (Dec 2022): findings that testing "should have been sequential was run in parallel," scope reduced "without appropriately skilled oversight and approval," testing concluded "the day before migration"; expectations: "better to miss a go live date than not be able to deliver a service," independent external "stress testing" of plans, a "cooling down period" before go/no-go, rollback planning — [Technology's Legal Edge](https://www.technologyslegaledge.com/2023/01/managing-migration-and-go-live-risk-lessons-from-the-tsb-sabadell-migration/); primary notices: [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/final-notices/tsb-bank-plc-2022.pdf), [PRA (bank)](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/prudential-regulation/regulatory-action/final-notice-from-pra-to-tsb-bank.pdf), [PRA (former CIO, personally fined)](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/prudential-regulation/regulatory-action/final-notice-from-pra-to-former-tsb-bank-plc-cio.pdf)
- FCA operational resilience observations (Mar 2026): firms must show "testing plans that show they can remain within impact tolerances," documented methodologies, annual reassessment "following material business changes," board approval; the paper "does not provide detailed requirements for change management processes, technology migration protocols, or staged testing" — [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-and-poor-practice/operational-resilience-insights-observations-one-year)
- US agencies overhauled model risk management guidance in Apr 2026 (parallel testing is a model-risk concept, not system-conversion) — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/Agencies-Overhaul-Model-Risk-Management-Guidance-for-Banks-Heres-What-Changed) (title only; not fetched)
- Infosys markets "compliance and auditability focus for regulated industries" as a differentiator, implying buyers ask for it — [IN-COM](https://www.in-com.com/blog/top-cobol-modernization-vendors-in-2025-2026-from-legacy-to-cloud/)
- OCC's "Conversions" licensing manual concerns charter conversions, not IT — [OCC 2004 (older, irrelevant)](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/DownloadDocument?objectID=5537601)

### Inferences
- The regulator asks for *evidence of adequate, independent, documented testing of calculations and controls*, which a byte-for-byte differential run over production inputs satisfies more cheaply and more defensibly than hand-written UAT. "Byte-for-byte on production inputs" is the vendor's phrasing (Mechanical Orchard, Heirloom), not the regulator's; the sale is to translate one into the other for the audit file.
- Line-level provenance maps directly onto FFIEC's "audit trails included in the application" language and onto SOX ITGC change-management evidence, which is a stronger regulatory hook than "idiomatic Java."
- The PRA's personal fine on TSB's former CIO means the individual buyer (CIO) bears personal regulatory risk for test adequacy, which is an unusual and strong incentive to buy proof rather than promises.

### Gaps
- Could not access FFIEC DA&M booklet text, OCC Comptroller's Handbook IT booklets, BaFin BAIT/MaRisk, or PRA SS text to quote conversion-testing language verbatim.
- No source found where a regulator names "parallel run" or "byte-for-byte" as required for core batch replacement; this appears to be industry practice, not written rule.
- SOX ITGC auditor expectations for code-conversion evidence: not researched in the time available.
