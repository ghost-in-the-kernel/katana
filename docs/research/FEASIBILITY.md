# Deterministic COBOL-to-Java compiler clears the feasibility bar

**Go, with the scope pinned to batch and the pitch pinned to proof.** Everything the proposed compiler needs that could be tested in this environment was tested, and it held: GnuCOBOL installed from Ubuntu's repository in about a minute and ran the public-domain NIST CCVS85 suite to **426 programs, 9,610 passes, 0 failures in 96 seconds** on two vCPUs, matching GnuCOBOL's shipped expected summary exactly; four AWS CardDemo batch programs ran end to end against five emulated VSAM files and produced **byte-identical datasets across repeated runs** once the clock was pinned to the hundredth of a second; the MIT-licensed ProLeap grammar parsed **31/31 CardDemo and 79/82 BankDemo programs**; and opensourcecobol4j demonstrated that a byte-image literal lowering of COBOL to Java compiles, runs, and matches `cobc` output byte for byte, with a provenance comment on every statement. About **760 programs are oracle-ready today** (400 NIST with reference reports, 359 GnuCOBOL autotests, a handful of textbook programs), and CardDemo's 12 batch programs are oracle-generatable in a day. The open-source landscape has no production parser for IBM dialect plus CICS, no JCL/DFSORT interpreter of any maturity, and no translator anyone would call maintainable; the commercial landscape claims "functional equivalence" universally and publishes audited pass/fail numbers nowhere, while Gartner has just told buyers that more than 70% of 2026 mainframe exits will fail because GenAI was oversold. That is the gap: no incumbent, hyperscaler or IBM emits the equivalence oracle and line-level provenance as compiler artifacts, and IBM's own researchers write that formal equivalence for COBOL-to-Java is out of reach. The product that fits a one-engineer-plus-agents team is therefore not "idiomatic Java" but **JOBOL you can prove, refactored in steps each shown output-identical**, with the oracle harness as the first deliverable and the first thing to sell. The principal risks are that GnuCOBOL is a stand-in for IBM semantics (intermediate precision, TRUNC, EBCDIC collation, hexadecimal float), that the IBM Enterprise COBOL manuals were unreachable from here and must be re-fetched before the numeric runtime is frozen, that the harness needs a DFSORT subset nobody has written in the open, and that CICS, DB2 and JCL are where every open tool stops.

## A redistributable corpus of 1.2 million unique lines exists, and 760 programs already carry oracles

The study cloned 24 repositories (5,127 COBOL files, 3,026,979 lines, 901 copybooks, 213 JCL members, 1.1 GB) into `/home/claude/cobol-research/corpus/`, with `MANIFEST.md` recording each repo's URL, license, counts and oracle-ready paths and `scan.sh` regenerating the per-repo feature table `scan.tsv`. After subtracting the five copies of the NIST suite that ride inside other projects, roughly **1.2 million lines are unique**, weighted toward four sources: the CNAF production batch programs in TypeCobol's test tree (about 333k lines, five Install/1-generated programs from 2,630 to **213,838 lines**, CeCILL-C), NIST CCVS85 (about 350k lines, public domain), the GnuCOBOL contrib tree (250k lines of GPL tools), and the two realistic business applications, AWS CardDemo (44 programs, 30,175 lines, Apache-2.0) and Rocket's BankDemo (82 programs, 20,670 lines, but licensed "solely for internal demonstration purposes with other Rocket products") ([CardDemo](https://github.com/aws-samples/aws-mainframe-modernization-carddemo); [BankDemo LICENSE](https://github.com/RocketSoftwareCOBOLandMainframe/BankDemo/blob/main/LICENSE.txt); [TypeCobol](https://github.com/TypeCobolTeam/TypeCobol)). No government COBOL application source was found on GitHub; the searches return only toys and news stories, and the largest open real application, ACAS at about 500,000 lines, lives only on SourceForge, which the proxy refused ([ACAS on SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/projects/acas/)).

The hard features are present in volume, which matters because a compiler proves itself on what breaks naive translation, not on HELLO WORLD. Per `scan.tsv`, GO TO appears 21,983 times in NIST, 1,922 in contrib and 392 in BankDemo; PERFORM THRU 2,546 and 1,987 times; COMP-3 about 65,000 times in the CNAF programs; REDEFINES 4,597 times in NIST and 6,257 in TypeCobol; EXEC CICS 240 times in CardDemo and 204 in BankDemo; EXEC SQL 277 in contrib and 122 in BankDemo. **ALTER occurs only in the NIST OB/NC modules (193 hits) and nowhere realistic**, so a compiler can treat it as legacy and still cover every realistic sample here. CardDemo's 12 batch programs have zero CICS and zero SQL, read VSAM KSDS files with alternate keys, and ship inputs in both EBCDIC and ASCII (50 accounts, 50 cards, 300 daily transactions and so on), which makes them the correct first realistic target ([CardDemo README](https://github.com/aws-samples/aws-mainframe-modernization-carddemo/blob/main/README.md)).

The oracle-ready count is the number that decides phase 1. The `eval-nist-cobol85` package holds **400 GnuCOBOL-generated NIST REPORT logs**, 388 with PASS lines, generated with GnuCOBOL 3.2.0 and released Apache-2.0 over public-domain NIST material ([eval-nist-cobol85 NOTICE](https://github.com/AnyEvalOrg/eval-nist-cobol85/blob/main/NOTICE.md)). This was checked locally: `cobc -x -std=cobol85 NC101A.CBL && ./NC101A` under GnuCOBOL 3.1.2 wrote a 146-line REPORT with 96 PASS and 4 FAIL lines that diffed to zero lines against `reference/NC/NC101A.log` after trailing-space stripping. GnuCOBOL's own test tree adds **359 autotest programs with byte-exact expected stdout** embedded in `.at` files, each isolating one semantic rule (edited MOVE, truncation, ROUNDED, ODO), which makes them the right unit corpus for proving refactoring passes ([GnuCOBOL tests](https://github.com/OCamlPro/gnucobol/tree/gcos4gnucobol-3.x/tests/testsuite.src)). The NIST programs are self-checking, so a mistranslation shows up twice: as a FAIL line in the report and as a byte difference in it. The one gap that cannot be closed from public material is that **no realistic multi-thousand-line program ships expected output**; every realistic oracle must be generated by running the COBOL under GnuCOBOL, which ties equivalence to GnuCOBOL's semantics rather than IBM's until a customer's mainframe output is in hand.

A redistributable feasibility corpus is therefore CardDemo plus NIST with the eval-nist reference logs plus the GnuCOBOL autotests plus ProLeap's ~300 MIT-licensed feature programs: everything the oracle argument needs is Apache, MIT or public domain. GPL material (contrib, opensourcecobol4j's tests) is fine as test input and must stay out of any published bundle; BankDemo stays private study.

## The oracle runs on apt-installed tooling in 96 seconds, but pinning is the real work

The equivalence oracle is buildable from open tooling and was substantially built during the study; the artifacts under `/home/claude/cobol-research/tools/` are a working prototype of each part. `apt-get update && apt-get install -y gnucobol3` yields GnuCOBOL 3.1.2 with the BDB indexed-file handler, GMP arithmetic and the full set of dialect configurations (`ibm.conf`, `mvs.conf`, `cobol85.conf`, each with a `-strict` variant). The NIST run is reproducible from `/home/claude/cobol-research/tools/nist/`: EXEC85 extracted from `newcob.val` (28,210,031 bytes, first line `CCVS85 VERSION 4.0 01 OCT 1992`), per-module `EXEC85.conf`, `expand.pl` and `report.pl` from the GnuCOBOL 3.x mirror, and the result in `summary.log` reads `Total 426 426 0 0 9610 0 20 118 9748`: 426 executed, 0 errors, 0 crashes, **9,610 pass, 0 fail**, 20 deliberately deleted by the harness configuration, 118 visual-inspection items (mostly SQ printer-format tests), with `diff` against GnuCOBOL's shipped `summary.txt` empty ([Zaneham/nist-cobol85-test-suite](https://github.com/Zaneham/nist-cobol85-test-suite); [GnuCOBOL cobol85 README](https://fossies.org/linux/gnucobol/tests/cobol85/README)). Two harness fixes were needed and neither was a compiler fault: `summary.pl` takes module names, and the 3.x `report.pl` passes `-Wno-goto-section`, unknown to 3.1.2, for IC115A. The GCC COBOL front-end maintainers' assessment that GnuCOBOL is "the single one that nearly passes everything" is borne out ([gcc-patches thread](https://www.mail-archive.com/gcc-patches@gcc.gnu.org/msg368220.html)). The caveat is that the suite tests the 1985 core: no IBM extensions, no CICS, DB2 or JCL, and it was run with `-std=cobol85`, not `-std=ibm`, so whether all 9,610 still pass under the IBM dialect is unknown and should be the first thing phase 0 measures.

CardDemo is where the pinning list came from. Of ten batch programs compiled with `cobc -x -std=ibm -I app/cpy`, seven compiled clean; `CBACT04C` and `CBSTM03B` take a JCL PARM (`PROCEDURE DIVISION USING`) and need `-m` or a driver, and `CBSTM03A` fails inside `CUSTREC.cpy`, which contains tab characters in the indicator area. The POSTTRAN job (`CBTRN02C`, 300 daily transactions against four KSDS files) ran to `TRANSACTIONS PROCESSED :000000300 / REJECTED :000000043`, return code 4, 257 records written. The first two runs with `COB_CURRENT_DATE="2024-03-01 12:00:00"` produced identical reject, account and balance files but **TRANFILE differed in every record at column 326**: that setting pins only to the second and `FUNCTION CURRENT-DATE` leaked live hundredths into a persisted timestamp. `COB_CURRENT_DATE="2024-03-01 12:00:00.00 +00:00"` with `TZ=UTC` made the logical unload identical across runs (`aed61744…`). Even then the raw BDB `.ksds` files differed in 7 bytes of page metadata, so **indexed files must be compared on a key-ordered logical unload, never on the physical file** (`tools/runs/carddemo/unload.cbl`, 35 lines). The TRANREPT job needed its JCL SORT step emulated with a 50-line COBOL `SORT ... WITH DUPLICATES IN ORDER` (`sortstep.cbl`), after which the sorted intermediate, the 60,914-byte report and stdout were identical across two complete runs and the report's grand total of 123,816.50 reconciled with the 257 posted transactions. `CBACT01C` aborted calling an **assembler** date routine (`app/asm/COBDATFT.asm`), and all four programs end errors with `CALL 'CEE3ABD'`; a 10-line stub (`CEE3ABD.cbl`) made abends observable and showed that `U999` surfaces as process exit 231 (999 mod 256), so the harness must capture the abend code itself. Loading `DISCGRP` as shipped gave file status 21 on 49 of 51 records because the ASCII flat file is not in key order; IDCAMS REPRO would reject it too.

That list, each item of which broke something here, is the oracle's job-fixture contract: compiler version and dialect flags pinned by image digest; every `ASSIGN` bound through `DD_<name>` (GnuCOBOL's `cob_chk_file_mapping` checks `DD_x`, `dd_x`, `x`, then `COB_FILE_PATH`, per `libcob/fileio.c`); `COB_CURRENT_DATE` with hundredths and zone plus `TZ`; `COB_LIBRARY_PATH` as STEPLIB; stubs for LE and assembler callees; `SYSIN` as stdin bytes and `PARM` via a driver; record formats per DD (`COB_VARSEQ_FORMAT`, `COB_LS_FIXED`); initial state of every indexed file loaded in key order; and the rule that **the original lane runs twice and the harness refuses to compare against Java until the original is self-identical**. GnuCOBOL's own SORT proved deterministic and stable on 20,000 records with 10 distinct keys, including with `COB_SORT_MEMORY=2M` forcing the disk-spill path; DFSORT is not guaranteed stable without `EQUALS`, so neither translator nor oracle may assume stability was intended.

Encoding is the place to be stubborn rather than clever. `cobc --info` reports `native EBCDIC: no`; the overpunch probe (`tools/runs/carddemo/ovp.cbl`) showed `PIC S9(5) VALUE -12345` stored as `31 32 33 34 75` by default and `31 32 33 34 4E` with `-fsign=EBCDIC`, where a mainframe holds `F1 F2 F3 F4 D5`; COMP-3 (`12 34 5D`) and big-endian COMP are codepage-independent in both. The collation probe showed `"a" < "A" < "1"` under an EBCDIC `PROGRAM COLLATING SEQUENCE` and the reverse without, and the same flag governs SORT order. The Open Mainframe Project course data file is EBCDIC with embedded COMP-3 fields, and a byte-blind `iconv` corrupts the packed fields; a 15-line field-aware transcoder (cp037 for `PIC X`, pass-through for `COMP-3`) was needed to get `17891797   $10,000.00      $188.74WASHINGTON` out. The rule that follows: normalize only text encoding, sign-zone schemes, RDW prefixes, line terminators and declared header positions, always with the copybook in hand; **refuse to normalize collation-dependent results, rounding, truncation, COMP-3 bytes, record counts and return codes**, because differences there are bugs in the Java, not representation artefacts. Best practice is two lanes in one encoding: the Java lane emits mainframe-faithful bytes, and in the GnuCOBOL development loop both lanes run `-fsign=EBCDIC` with an EBCDIC collating sequence. The one external data point agrees: the "Zero Variance" team that ran COBOL against Java on an emulated mainframe still needed `BigDecimal` with `HALF_UP` to match packed arithmetic, and compared at the database layer rather than byte for byte ([dev.to: Zero Variance](https://dev.to/banuap/zero-variance-proving-cobol-to-java-semantic-equivalence-with-a-live-mainframe-emulator-on-aws-1ani)).

What GnuCOBOL does not cover is the job. It binds DD names, sorts and handles return codes, but knows nothing of steps, `COND`, `DISP`, GDGs, concatenation, `SYSOUT`, IDCAMS, IEBGENER or DFSORT control cards. The GitHub JCL topic under the COBOL filter holds only course material ([GitHub topics: jcl](https://github.com/topics/jcl?l=cobol)); the gnucobol-users list names only commercial interpreters and converters ([gnucobol-users 2015](https://lists.gnu.org/archive/html/gnucobol-users/2015-07/msg00015.html)); the one open attempt, `mainframe-frontier`, is a Rust JES-style runner over GnuCOBOL 3.2 that supports JOB/EXEC/DD/COND, GDGs, a DFSORT subset and IDCAMS but not PROCs, IF/THEN/ELSE, concatenation, VSAM or restart, with 0 stars and 5 commits ([roberjo/mainframe-frontier](https://github.com/roberjo/mainframe-frontier)). CardDemo's JCL needed only PGM, DD DSN/DISP, IDCAMS DEFINE/REPRO/DELETE, SORT FIELDS/INCLUDE, GDG (+1), PROC and SYSOUT, which is a bounded subset to own. A Hercules + MVS 3.8j lane is nearly free (Docker images exist) and gives real JES2, VSAM and EBCDIC as a third opinion on IBM semantics, but its compiler is OS/VS COBOL (1974 standard, no `END-IF`, no inline PERFORM, no intrinsics) and the Hercules FAQ calls MVS 3.8's legal status "somewhat murky" while z/OS is "definitely licensed to a particular machine" ([SDL Hercules FAQ](https://sdl-hercules-390.github.io/html/hercfaq.html); [tk5-hercules](https://github.com/joergschultzelutter/tk5-hercules)). It is a validation lane, not the oracle.

The harness is three parts: a content-addressed job fixture (job model, datasets in declared encoding, copybooks, environment, optional mainframe gold), two runners sharing one DD-to-path contract, and a comparator that works on logical record streams and reports the first difference as dataset, record, byte offset, copybook field path, both byte values and decoded values, grouping repeats by field so one wrong date format is one finding over 257 records rather than 257 findings. Masks are declared, never inferred, and every mask applied is listed. The study's estimate for a usable v1 on GnuCOBOL alone is **7.5 to 9.5 engineer-weeks**, with the DFSORT subset (2 to 3 weeks and open-ended if INREC/OUTREC/OUTFIL grow) the largest single item, plus 1 to 2 weeks for a Hercules lane; running cost is negligible at about 100 seconds for the entire NIST suite. These figures are from this session's three hours of ad-hoc work and the component list, not from a published comparable, and should be treated as such.

## The literal lowering must be a byte-image machine, and its reference design already exists

The central technical finding is that the literal Java intermediate cannot be "Java with BigDecimal fields." It must be a byte-image machine: every data item a view (offset, length, PICTURE/USAGE descriptor) over a shared `byte[]` per 01-level record, arithmetic in arbitrary-precision decimal with the compiler's intermediate-truncation rule applied operation by operation, and control flow a paragraph dispatcher with PERFORM continuation semantics. This is exactly GnuCOBOL's libcob design (`cob_field` = pointer + size + attribute; `cob_decimal` = GMP integer + scale), and opensourcecobol4j's libcobj is a Java port of it that passes 8,804 of 8,918 NIST tests with zero failures, so the design is proven on the JVM ([GnuCOBOL numeric.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OCamlPro/gnucobol/gcos4gnucobol-3.x/libcob/numeric.c); [opensourcecobol4j](https://github.com/opensourcecobol/opensourcecobol4j)).

The catalogue of semantics the lowering must get exactly right, with the "wrong obvious Java" for each, is the specification for the runtime:

| Area | Rule the lowering must reproduce | What naive Java gets wrong | Executable reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Intermediate precision | IBM "dmax" rules: division held to max(d2−d1, dmax) decimals; `COMPUTE R = A / B * C` with A=1, B=3, C=300 gives **99.00**, exact arithmetic gives 99.99 | `divide(MathContext.DECIMAL128)` | GnuCOBOL `arithmetic-osvs: yes` in the IBM dialect; reproducer in a conformance bug report ([gitgalaxy #4287](https://github.com/squid-protocol/gitgalaxy/issues/4287)); IBM COBOL for Linux PG Appendix C ([IBM PDF](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS7FZ2_1.1.0/pdf/pglin.pdf)) |
| TRUNC option | STD truncates binaries to PICTURE digits, BIN to the 2/4/8-byte width, OPT is "dependent on the particular code sequence generated" | `short` wraps at 32767, matching neither | `binary-truncate`, `binary-size: 2-4-8` in `ibm-strict.conf`; worked examples ([ibmmainframes](https://ibmmainframes.com/about59717.html); [tek-tips](https://www.tek-tips.com/threads/trunc-opt.883811/)) |

| Sign encodings | Zoned overpunch differs by code page and compiler (EBCDIC `{`/A–I and `}`/J–R; GnuCOBOL/MF `p`–`y` in ASCII); `IS NUMERIC` on F-signed packed passes on IBM only | Parsing the digits as text | `hostsign: yes`; [Signed overpunch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_overpunch) |

| OCCURS DEPENDING ON | Items after an ODO table are variably located; sender uses current length, receiver with object inside uses maximum; contents beyond the count undefined | `List<E>` with a separate tail field | `odoslide: yes`, `complex-odo: yes`; IBM ODO clause ([IBM 6.4 LR](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/cobol-zos/6.4.0?topic=clause-occurs-depending)) |
| PERFORM / GO TO | PERFORM installs a continuation at a range end and falls through paragraphs textually; reaching any active range's exit returns (IBM); GO TO out of a range leaves it active; recursion undefined | One method per paragraph, `return` | `perform-osvs: yes`; IBM PERFORM rules ([IBM 6.4 LR](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.4.0/lr/ref/rlpsperf1.html)); the perform-frame stack in `cobc -C` output |

Two things in that table deserve emphasis. First, **compiler options are semantics**: TRUNC, NUMPROC, ARITH (18 versus 31 digits), code page and even the optimization level under TRUNC(OPT) change results, and IBM's own compiler changed its intermediate rounding between releases (APAR PH64935: 33 versus 31 decimal places under ARITH(EXTEND), and a stored value of 1573.6375999999999999 where 4.2 gave 1573.6376) ([IBM APAR PH64935](https://www.ibm.com/support/pages/apar/PH64935)). A "pinned environment" claim that does not name compiler version and these options is not reproducible, and the lowering must emit them as a manifest in the Java output. Second, some behaviours cannot be made exact by construction and must become **compile-time findings rather than silent lowerings**: TRUNC(OPT) programs; reliance on overlapping PERFORM ranges or recursive PERFORM; division by zero without SIZE ERROR; ODO data beyond the current count; COMP-1/COMP-2 hexadecimal float bit patterns; reads of uninitialized storage. This is the same shape as Suite Builder's site findings: what is wrong with the input is said first.

The control-flow literature settles how structuring should be staged. On an 8 MLOC banking system with 531,351 GOTOs, pattern rewriting removed 21%, a Böhm–Jacopini program-counter switch removed 79% in 22 seconds with mean nesting +46%, and the Erosa–Hendren algorithm removed 100% at the cost of mean nesting +232% to +381% and 117 to 144 hours, which the authors judged "mission impossible" to maintain ([Ceccato et al., CSMR 2008](https://profs.scienze.univr.it/~ceccato/papers/2008/csmr2008.pdf)). For a deterministic compiler with a golden, the Böhm–Jacopini dispatcher **is** the literal intermediate, `for(;;) switch(pc)` with a PERFORM continuation table, and structuring is a refactoring pass with a proof obligation: a paragraph set becomes a method only when it is entered solely by PERFORM, exits only through its range end, is not an ALTER target and is not recursive, all of which are checkable CFG properties on the lowered dispatcher. The same logic governs every other refactoring: a record collapses to a typed class only when alias analysis finds no REDEFINES, RENAMES, reference modification, ODO, group MOVE or raw I/O touching it; step-wise IBM arithmetic becomes exact arithmetic only when the proof shows no division or non-integer exponent can trigger dmax truncation; a subprogram becomes stateless only when every WORKING-STORAGE item is written before read on every path. Each pass is then provable in two ways at once: the golden corpus output stays byte-identical and the paragraph-entry trace stays equal.

GnuCOBOL's `cobc -C` output and opensourcecobol4j's `cobj -C` output are the reference design for all of this and were examined side by side on a 42-line program exercising packed decimal, edited pictures, REDEFINES, OCCURS, 88-levels, PERFORM THRU, PERFORM VARYING, COMPUTE ROUNDED and a GO TO loop (`/home/claude/cobol-research/tools/runs/oc4j/CONSTR.{cbl,java,c}`). The C has a static byte buffer and a `cob_field {size, data, attr}` per item, a `/* Line: N : VERB : file */` comment per statement, `d0..dn` decimal scratch registers, and a perform-frame stack with the "implicit PERFORM return" check at the end of every THRU target. The Java is a near-mechanical transliteration (`CobolDataStorage`, `CobolField`, `CobolDecimal`, a `CobolControl[]` label trampoline where GO TO is `return Optional.of(contList[target])`), compiles with `javac` against the hand-built runtime in `tools/oc4j-rt/`, and prints seven `TOTAL:` lines **byte-identical to `cobc -x`**. CBACT01C's 430 lines became 1,826 lines of Java, a 4.2× expansion that matches Sneed's reported 2× for a less literal scheme ([Sneed 2010](https://fb-swt.gi.de/fileadmin/FB/SWT/Softwaretechnik-Trends/Verzeichnis/Band_30_Heft_2/17-sneed.pdf)). Both tools are GPL, so their **design is the specification and their code stays out**; their outputs, however, are free to use as oracles, diffing data layouts (`b_N` sizes and offsets, attribute tuples) and run results against the new back end on every program it accepts.

## No open tool parses IBM COBOL with CICS, but the ProLeap grammar plus che4z's CICS grammar would

Four parsers and one translator were built from source without any package manager (the proxy blocked Maven Central, Gradle, SourceForge and GitHub release assets; the ANTLR 4.13.2 jar came out of the npm package `antlr4ng-cli`, the tree-sitter runtime from PyPI, Ant from Gradle's lib) and run over CardDemo, the Open Mainframe Project course, BankDemo and NIST. Per-file results are in `/home/claude/cobol-research/tools/results/`, the drivers are `tools/proleap-cobol-parser/build/Driver.java`, `tools/runs/cobj-run.sh` and `tools/tree-sitter-cobol/run_ts.py`, the CICS copybook stubs (DFHAID, DFHBMSCA, which IBM supplies and the repos omit) are in `tools/stubs/`, and the normalized NIST source (selection codes in column 7 rewritten, `XXXXX0nn` ASSIGN placeholders quoted as the CCVS user guide intends) is `tools/nist/src2`.

| GnuCOBOL 3.1.2 `cobc -fsyntax-only` | 7 of 10 batch | 2 of 5 tried | — | 264 | The reference; rejects the same IBM idioms as cobj |

The NIST column needs reading with care. The remaining ~180 failures shared by every tool are test-suite artefacts, not grammar gaps: 76 are the `XXXXX069` placeholder standing in an FD clause, 31 are `LABEL RECORDS ARE "XXXXX0nn"`, 25 are the hyphenated `RECORD-KEY` spelling that `cobc` also rejects, and 13 are indicator codes U/H/E. **Of ProLeap's 180 NIST failures, zero are programs GnuCOBOL 3.1.2 accepts.** Per module ProLeap passes NC 87/95, IF 45/45, IC 45/47 and SQ 54/85 but RL 2/35, ST 5/40 and RW 0/6, where the placeholders cluster. opensourcecobol4j's own "8,804 pass, 0 fail" claim is a runtime result after the CCVS driver resolves those placeholders, and the translate-only 263/459 here does not contradict it ([opensourcecobol4j README](https://github.com/opensourcecobol/opensourcecobol4j)).

On mainframe-style code the picture is sharper. ProLeap's three BankDemo failures are one grammar gap (`ALTERNATE KEY` without `RECORD`, which Micro Focus and IBM accept); its OMP failures are one deliberately broken lab program and one grammar gap (`FUNCTION TRIM(x, TRAILING)` keyword arguments); its initial 14/31 on CardDemo rose to 31/31 after stubbing the IBM copybooks and working around `LiteralCopyBookFinderImpl`, which matches a quoted `COPY 'NAME'` against the file path exactly and never applies `copyBookExtensions` ([uwol/proleap-cobol-parser](https://github.com/uwol/proleap-cobol-parser)). The real-world failure classes any new front end must handle, all observed in this corpus, are: tabs in the indicator area; `PROGRAM-ID. X` without a period (19 of 30 course programs, accepted by Enterprise COBOL, rejected by the entire OpenCOBOL lineage); `ALTERNATE KEY`; keyword arguments to intrinsic functions; `NOT=` as one token; `LENGTH OF` inside a reference modifier; BMS map copybooks with 01-level REDEFINES-of-REDEFINES and REDEFINES larger than the target; quoted COPY names; and missing IBM-supplied copybooks that the tool must synthesize.

The CICS problem is the dividing line. ProLeap tokenizes `EXEC CICS ... END-EXEC` line by line as text; Koopa parses it into command/option/value but cannot be trusted elsewhere; cobj rejects it; tree-sitter breaks on it. The only large open CICS and DB2 grammars are che4z's (`CICSParser.g4` 5,289 lines, `Db2SqlExecParser.g4` 1,659 lines, EPL-2.0, IBM/Broadcom lineage, last commit the day before this study), whose COBOL grammars generated cleanly with the 4.13.2 jar but were not driven over the corpus for lack of time ([eclipse-che4z](https://github.com/eclipse-che4z/che-che4z-lsp-for-cobol)). Spantree's `tree-sitter-cobol-enterprise` self-reports 65/66 CardDemo files clean but 6.3% on NIST, and could not be generated here ([Spantree](https://github.com/Spantree/tree-sitter-cobol-enterprise)).

The reuse recommendation is to reuse a grammar, not a pipeline: **fork ProLeap's two ANTLR grammars as the base, graft che4z's CICS and DB2 grammars as sub-parsers over ProLeap's EXEC text tokens**, fix the small list above, add an IBM dialect setting and a permissive indicator-column policy, and build the compiler's own IR directly from the ANTLR tree rather than carrying ProLeap's 1,119-file ASG layer. Koopa's silent skipping and tree-sitter's error recovery are each disqualifying for a deterministic compiler; cb2xml, JRecord and Cobrix cover the data division only (though JRecord's cp037 handling and Cobrix's RDW/ODO modes are useful references for the comparator). The che4z verification is about 30 minutes of work and the EPL-2.0 compatibility question is a legal judgment still to make.

## Incumbents sell a process promise; none emits proof as a compiler artifact

The market divides into three camps and an emerging fourth. The LLM-assisted camp is IBM's watsonx Code Assistant for Z, now converging with its RPG sibling into "Project Bob" using primarily Claude Sonnet 4.5 alongside Granite, Llama and Mistral, with GA planned for 2026 ([IT Jungle](https://www.itjungle.com/2025/10/13/big-blue-converges-ibm-i-rpg-and-system-z-cobol-code-assistants-into-project-bob/); [CROZ](https://croz.net/honest-take-on-watsonx-code-assistant-for-z/)); AWS Transform for mainframe, which since December 2025 offers assessment and transformation "at no cost to AWS customers" plus generated test plans and data-collection JCL that the customer's own sysprogs must run ([AWS News Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/aws-transform-for-mainframe-introduces-reimagine-capabilities-and-automated-testing-functionality/)); and Google's Gemini-based Mainframe Rewrite with Dual Run, Santander's "Gravity" productized, which replays production events against the new system and diffs outcomes for months before flipping primary ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/infrastructure-modernization/accelerate-mainframe-modernization-with-google-cloud-ai)). The rule-based camp (Heirloom, TSRI, CloudFrame, mLogica, the acquired Blu Age, Astadia, Asysco and Advanced) claims "100% automated" and "functional equivalence guaranteed"; Heirloom argues deterministic transpilation gives "byte-for-byte equivalence" by construction and cites a 64-million-line PHEAA migration that "succeeded on the first attempt" with no disclosed test methodology ([Heirloom](https://heirloomcomputing.com/llms-and-cobol-modernization/); [PHEAA case](https://heirloomcomputing.com/portfolio/pheaa/)). The keep-COBOL camp (Rocket, which paid $2.28B for OpenText's Micro Focus business; Veryant; Raincode) argues against translating at all ([Rocket Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Software)). The verification-first camp is Mechanical Orchard ($84M+ raised, a byte-for-byte gate against live data on the actual LPAR, no independent audit of its claims) and Hypercubic ($5.3M seed, August 2026) ([HFS Research](https://www.hfsresearch.com/research/hfs-services-as-software-hot-tech-mechanical-orchard/); [Hypercubic](https://www.hypercubic.ai/insights/hypercubic-raises-usd5-3m-to-build-the-future-of-mainframe-modernization)).

Across all four camps, **nobody publishes audited pass/fail numbers**. IBM Research's own evaluation of WCA4Z's COBOL-to-Java subsystem grades output by syntactic checks, loose semantic matching, skeleton compilation and an LLM-as-judge on a 7-point scale, and "presents no aggregate correctness rates or failure percentages" ([arXiv 2507.23356](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23356v1)); a November 2025 practitioner review says the Java "requires significant manual clean up" ([CROZ](https://croz.net/honest-take-on-watsonx-code-assistant-for-z/)). A Blu Age user review advises the tool be "avoided for the financial domain applications" because results can differ after conversion ([TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/mainframe-modernization-with-aws-blu-age/reviews)). The only vendor-independent number a buyer will have heard is Gartner's June 2026 forecast that **more than 70% of mainframe exit projects initiated in 2026 will fail to produce intended benefits** and that 75% of exit vendors will pivot or cease by 2030, because of "the widening gap between the marketing promise of GenAI and its real-world ability to transform and migrate complex legacy code" ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-06-18-gartner-predicts-more-than-70-percent-of-mainframe-exit-projects-will-fail-due-to-overestimation-of-generative-ais-capabilities)). "Functional equivalence" in this market is a process promise (we will test until it matches), not a compiler guarantee, and every incumbent bolts its equivalence evidence on as a separate harness. **No incumbent found advertises line-level provenance from COBOL to Java as a first-class output.** A compiler that emits the oracle fixture, the diff report and the provenance map as build artifacts is different in kind, not degree.

Pricing says where a small vendor can and cannot win. Translation is now a loss leader for cloud consumption (AWS, Google) or consulting (IBM); conversion services run $1.50 to $4.00 per line and enterprise platforms $2.2M over 12 to 24 months; ISG notes buyers commonly use two or three vendor tools per portfolio and that per-line pricing "can become expensive" while runtime fees "accumulate after modernization" ([ISG 2024](https://pages.awscloud.com/rs/112-TZM-766/images/24-GLOBAL-en-US-isg-other-ardm-isg-mainframe-application-modernization-software-reprint.pdf); [IN-COM](https://www.in-com.com/blog/top-cobol-modernization-vendors-in-2025-2026-from-legacy-to-cloud/)). A small team cannot win on the price of translation. It can win on the price of **assurance**, sold to risk and audit, or as the engine an SI resells. The regulatory hook is real: FFIEC asks auditors to review "testing, and audit trails included in the application" with "extensive testing of program logic, calculations, error conditions" ([FFIEC IT Handbook](https://ithandbook.ffiec.gov/it-booklets/audit/audit-participation-in-application-development,-acquisition,-conversions,-and-testing.aspx)), and the TSB enforcement (£48.65M fine plus redress, with the former CIO fined personally) cited reduced test scope, parallelized phases and testing concluded "the day before migration" ([Technology's Legal Edge](https://www.technologyslegaledge.com/2023/01/managing-migration-and-go-live-risk-lessons-from-the-tsb-sabadell-migration/)). A byte-for-byte differential run over production-shaped inputs satisfies that language more cheaply and more defensibly than hand-written UAT, and line-level provenance maps onto "audit trails included in the application" directly. No regulator names "byte-for-byte" or "parallel run"; that is vendor vocabulary, and the sale is translating one into the other for the audit file.

On IBM's radar: the IBM/Advanced deal of January 2024, in which IBM Consulting bought a team with a proven rule-based toolchain and reference customers and wrapped it around WCA4Z, is the template ([CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/ibm-buys-advanced-mainframe-modernization-unit/705078/)). IBM Z just had "the highest annual revenue for Z in about 20 years" and IBM protects the platform aggressively, including the March 2025 UK High Court win against LzLabs ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/29/ibm_q4_2025/); [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchitoperations/news/366620383/Analysts-IBM-LzLabs-suit-a-cautionary-tale-for-IT-buyers)). WCA4Z's two modes, refactor to Java or maintain COBOL without conversion, make it a retention tool as much as a migration tool. A small deterministic engine that **verifies** LLM-generated Java, an independent equivalence oracle for Bob's output, complements IBM's strategy rather than competing with it, and can partner with all three hyperscalers, whose open need is testing (AWS added it in December 2025; Google has Dual Run), without competing with any. That is the credible path onto the radar; a head-on "better translator than IBM" pitch is not.

## The literature names the gap, and the failures show what proof cannot cover

IBM's own papers are the most serious published work on checking COBOL-to-Java translations and they concede the exact ground this product would occupy. The quality-evaluation paper states that formal equivalence is treated as out of reach because "the semantic gap between COBOL and Java [is] too significant," that dynamic testing "requires access to Z platforms configured correctly" and "has yielded the lowest return on investment," and that paragraph-level LLM translation compounds error: "if a program has 10 paragraphs and each is translated with 90% accuracy, the probability of a fully correct program is only about 34%" ([arXiv 2507.23356](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23356v1)). The ASE 2024 validation tool generates inputs by symbolic execution, runs the COBOL under a debugger on a real Z with resources mocked, and turns outputs into JUnit assertions; on nine programs of 300 to 1,500 lines one case went 0/2 and exposed a real loop-condition inversion ([arXiv 2506.10999](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.10999)). The 2025 follow-up reached 92.8% full branch coverage over an internal benchmark of 54,284 programs and names the recurring transformation bug classes: logic inversion (UNTIL versus while), parameter scope loss, SQLCODE branches replaced by exceptions, and fixed-length padding versus Java strings ([arXiv 2504.10548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10548)). Nobody in the literature has applied compiler translation validation (the Alive2 pattern of per-transformation checking) to COBOL migration ([Alive2, PLDI 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3453483.3454030)). The two IBM positions together are the opening: IBM's "lowest ROI" verdict on dynamic testing is about LLM paragraph translation needing a Z box, and a literal-Java intermediate that runs off-mainframe against a GnuCOBOL lane changes that economics; IBM's "formal equivalence is infeasible" is true of the COBOL-to-Java front end and false of the refactoring passes, which are Java-to-Java and checkable per pass.

The deterministic tradition produced working but COBOL-shaped output and no proof either. Terekhov and Verhoef in 2000 catalogued six difficulty classes (type idiosyncrasies, the "homonym problem," operations tied to internal representation such as STRING and INSPECT, cumulative compensatory "alien code"), called the result "Java-compliant COBOL programs," reported three companies bankrupted and one $50M write-off from failed conversions, and recommended restructuring in the source before swapping syntax ([Terekhov & Verhoef](https://www.cs.vu.nl/~x/cnv/)). Sneed's 2010 COB2Java converted 200,711 lines with 15 rules into Java about twice the size, with data as char arrays in singletons and GOTO as a finite-state-machine controller, and reported no regression results ([Sneed 2010](https://fb-swt.gi.de/fileadmin/FB/SWT/Softwaretechnik-Trends/Verzeichnis/Band_30_Heft_2/17-sneed.pdf)). "JOBOL," attributed to Jason Bloomberg in 2022 trade press, is a marketing word for this 25-year-old observation, and no source anywhere gives measured maintainability or defect numbers for it ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchsoftwarequality/news/252523671/COBOL-programming-skills-gap-thwarts-modernization-to-Java); [Intellyx, CloudFrame-sponsored](https://intellyx.com/2022/07/16/modernizing-your-mainframe-cobol-beware-the-jobol-pitfall/)). The literature treats the literal shape as the end product; the proposed compiler treats it as a pinned golden to refactor away from, and would produce the first measured "refactoring distance" numbers in the field.

The failures teach what the proof does not cover, and the report should say so plainly. The one detailed COBOL-to-Java post-mortem, the New York Times Company's conversion of more than two million lines with Modern Systems' translator (ICSME 2018), says testing consumed 70 to 80% of the project and slipped it a year, and production still surfaced a 147-day date-field overflow, a batch-concurrency corruption from unplanned simultaneous jobs, and a performance collapse when one multi-layout VSAM file became 97 Oracle tables ([arXiv 1808.03724](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.03724)). TSB's 2018 failure came from two data centres "configured inconsistently" that nine dress rehearsals did not catch ([TSB release](https://www.tsb.co.uk/news-releases/slaughter-and-may.html)); Phoenix came from a cancelled pilot and "lack of true end-to-end testing" ([Phoenix pay system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_pay_system)). Equivalence proof covers the code-semantics slice. Environment configuration, data edge cases outside the corpus, scheduling and concurrency, I/O performance after a storage change, and governance are outside it, and an honest product says which slice it proves. The one independent practitioner artifact found reinforces where the proof earns its keep: its single blocking finding on an AI-migrated batch program was loss of fixed field width in edited-display output, with uncertainty about physical record encoding gating cutover "regardless of how clean the arithmetic looks" ([stephensjosef-cloud](https://github.com/stephensjosef-cloud/cobol-behavioral-equivalence)). A comparator that understands PIC clauses and physical encodings is exactly the differentiator.

The credible proof artifact borrows vocabulary from compiler qualification rather than inventing it: CompCert's DO-178C qualification experience and NASA's formal-methods tool-qualification report frame a translator's trustworthiness as a specification of both semantics, evidence per transformation step, and qualification data for the checker itself ([CompCert ERTS 2018](https://xavierleroy.org/publi/erts2018_compcert.pdf); [NASA/CR-2017-219371](https://shemesh.larc.nasa.gov/fm/FMinCert/NASA-CR-2017-219371.pdf)). Translated for a CTO or auditor: translation validation per pass for the refactoring stages, differential testing with coverage for the COBOL-to-literal-Java stage, and tool qualification for the comparator. The artifact must pin the COBOL compiler and version, ARITH/TRUNC/NUMPROC options, code page, the copybooks used to interpret every compared byte, the input corpus hash, the coverage achieved, and an explicit not-covered list.

## Go: phase 0 is the oracle, and every phase has a check that fails first

The recommendation is **go**, conditioned on three disciplines the team already has from Suite Builder: the oracle before the compiler, byte-identical goldens as the only definition of done, and findings before output for every construct the lowering cannot make exact. The product thesis to hold onto is narrow and defensible: a deterministic compiler whose first output is a pinned literal Java with a provenance comment per statement and a compile-options manifest, whose refactoring passes are each proven output-identical and trace-identical on the golden corpus, and whose oracle harness is itself a shippable tool that can verify anyone's Java, including IBM's. The thesis to drop is "idiomatic Java," which no one can prove and which Gartner has just discredited as a promise.

**Phase 0, oracle and corpus (the 7.5 to 9.5 engineer-weeks estimated above).** Pin GnuCOBOL 3.1.2 (or a 3.2 source build) in a container by digest; reproduce `Total 426 426 0 0 9610 0 20 118 9748` in CI from `tools/nist/`; re-run the suite under `-std=ibm` and record what changes; promote the ad-hoc CardDemo pieces (`load_*.cbl`, `unload.cbl`, `CEE3ABD.cbl`, `sortstep.cbl`, the field-aware transcoder) into the job-model harness with DD resolution, DISP/GDG/temp handling, IDCAMS/IEBGENER/IEFBR14, a DFSORT subset that fails loudly on anything unsupported, the double-run determinism gate, and the copybook-driven comparator; freeze the POSTTRAN and TRANREPT goldens (`aed61744…` for the TRANFILE unload, `a7d14a96…` and `19b7f04f…` for TRANREPT) and add INTCALC and CREASTMT once a PARM driver and the `CUSTREC.cpy` tab fix exist. In parallel, re-fetch IBM's Enterprise COBOL 6.x Programming Guide Appendix A (intermediate results), the TRUNC/NUMPROC/ARITH pages, the SYNC rules and the CICS translator output shape from an unblocked network; the study's dmax rules are corroborated by the COBOL for Linux appendix and a conformance bug report but the exact table wording was 403 from here. **Check:** the original lane is self-identical across two runs for every fixture; the NIST summary diff is empty; every CardDemo dataset hash is stable; the comparator's first-difference report names a copybook field for an injected one-byte change.

**Phase 1, front end and literal lowering on NIST.** Fork the ProLeap grammars; verify che4z's CICS and DB2 grammars on CardDemo and BankDemo (about 30 minutes) and decide the graft; fix the measured gaps (`ALTERNATE KEY`, keyword arguments to intrinsics, indicator-column policy, quoted COPY, IBM dialect switches mirroring `ibm-strict.conf`); build the IR with the attribute tuple, data map and dispatcher; write the byte-image runtime (Field views, Decimal with an `ArithProfile` of IBM_COMPAT/IBM_EXTEND/EXACT, store with ROUNDED/SIZE ERROR/TruncMode, EBCDIC-aware collation, status-returning file verbs). Lower NC and IF first (the nucleus and intrinsics are where ProLeap already passes 87/95 and 45/45), then SQ/RL/IX/ST. **Check:** the Java lane reproduces the same PASS/FAIL lines and counts as the 9,610 GnuCOBOL passes; the emitted data map matches `cobc -C`'s `b_N` sizes and offsets and attribute tuples on every accepted program; every statement in the Java carries its COBOL file and line; every construct from the non-exact list produces a finding, never silent output.

**Phase 2, CardDemo batch.** Add the indexed-file runtime (START/READ NEXT, alternate keys with duplicates in insertion order), SORT/MERGE with byte-image keys, CALL/LINKAGE with sticky linkage and run-unit RETURN-CODE, the LE and assembler stub facade, and PARM. **Check:** POSTTRAN, TRANREPT, INTCALC and CREASTMT produce byte-identical datasets, SYSOUT and return codes against the frozen goldens, compared on logical unloads for the KSDS files; the Java lane consumes the same `DD_*` fixture as the GnuCOBOL lane with no per-program adaptation.

**Phase 3, proven refactoring passes.** Implement the passes in order of provability: structuring by pattern rewrite and Böhm–Jacopini method extraction under the CFG conditions above; scalar promotion under alias analysis; exact-arithmetic substitution where no dmax truncation can fire; stateless subprograms under the written-before-read proof. **Check:** after every pass, every golden from phases 1 and 2 is byte-identical and the paragraph-entry trace is equal; each pass reports the refactoring distance it achieved and the obligations it could not discharge, which is a number no one in the field publishes.

**Phase 4, credibility and scale.** Parse and lower the CNAF CGMX02 program (213,838 lines) to prove the front end and data-map computation at production scale; run BankDemo privately for the CICS and SQL facade; stand up a Hercules/MVS 3.8j lane for true EBCDIC and DFSORT-era collation as a third opinion; publish the NIST and CardDemo results with the harness and fixtures under Apache-2.0; and approach the hyperscalers and IBM with the harness as an independent oracle for their LLM output, not as a competing translator.

The principal risks, in the order they would kill the project. **GnuCOBOL is a stand-in for IBM.** Its default division computes to 38 extra places where IBM holds dmax, its `-std=ibm` flags are a best-effort encoding of IBM behaviour, and hexadecimal float, NUMPROC detail and TRUNC(OPT) have no exact analogue; until a customer's z/OS output reconciles the two lanes on the same inputs, every equivalence claim is "equivalent to GnuCOBOL's reading of IBM semantics," and the product must say so. **CICS, DB2 and JCL are where every open tool stops**, and the DFSORT subset is open-ended; scope must stay on batch with facades until a paying shop names the subset it uses. **The IBM manuals were unreachable from this environment**, so the numeric runtime cannot be frozen on the evidence collected here. **Licensing** needs a judgment on EPL-2.0 grammar files inside an MIT or proprietary compiler, and the GPL code of `cobc` and `cobj` must never be copied. **The market gives translation away**; the revenue is in assurance and partnership, and getting on IBM's radar means complementing Project Bob, whose GA is planned for 2026, before it ships its own verification story. And the one risk the notes could not quantify: no source estimated the effort of the compiler itself as distinct from the oracle; opensourcecobol4j's runtime is 87 source files and NACA's Java runtime ran to about 153,000 lines, which brackets the runtime a literal lowering needs, but the front-end, IR and pass work is the team's own estimate to make ([InfoQ on NACA](https://www.infoq.com/news/2009/07/cobol-to-java/)).

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. Before it, the proposal read as "build a better COBOL-to-Java translator than IBM, AWS and Google," which a one-engineer team cannot do and which the market is tired of hearing. After it, the proposal reads as "build the oracle and provenance the whole market lacks, and let the compiler be the thing that is proven against it," which no incumbent does, which IBM's own researchers say they cannot do by proof, and which Gartner's 70% forecast has just made the question every CIO will be asked. The evidence that it is buildable is not argument but artifacts: a 96-second NIST run with zero failures, byte-identical CardDemo datasets once a hundredths-of-a-second clock leak was found and pinned, two grammars that between them parse the realistic corpus, and a GPL reference design that already runs COBOL semantics byte-exactly on the JVM.

The deeper implication is that the hard part is not translation but determinism, and determinism is a discipline the team already practises. The clock leak at column 326, the BDB page bytes that differ while the records do not, the EBCDIC overpunch that `iconv` destroys, the DISCGRP file that is not in key order: none of these is a compiler problem, and every one of them would have been a false "mismatch" or, worse, a false "match" in a harness that did not own them. Byte-identical goldens, fact-for-fact conformance and findings before output are exactly the habits that turn those into a product. The first thing to build is the first thing to sell, and it costs about two months.
